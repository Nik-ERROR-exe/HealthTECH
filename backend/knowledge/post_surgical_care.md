# Post-Surgical Care Guidelines

## Overview
After surgery, recovery depends on wound care, medication adherence, infection
awareness, pain management, and gradual resumption of daily activity. This guide
helps patients, caregivers, and monitoring agents recognise normal recovery
versus warning signs that require medical attention.

## Wound Care
- Keep the surgical wound clean and dry for the first 24 to 48 hours unless the
  surgeon instructs otherwise.
- Change dressings only as often as instructed, or when they become wet or
  soiled. Wash hands before and after each dressing change.
- Gently clean the wound with mild soap and water per instructions; do not rub.
  Pat dry with a clean towel.
- Mild redness and slight swelling around the incision line within the first few
  days is common. Expected healing signs include slight pink discolouration and
  small amounts of clear or pale-yellow drainage.
- Do not apply creams, ointments, powders, or traditional remedies to the wound
  unless prescribed by the treating doctor.

## Warning Signs (when to contact the doctor)
A patient should contact the doctor promptly if they notice any of the following:
- Increasing redness spreading beyond the incision, warmth of the surrounding
  skin, or new swelling.
- Wound drainage that is thick, green, yellow, or foul-smelling.
- Wound edges separating (gaping open) or wound feels increasingly painful.
- Fever above 38°C (100.4°F), chills, or generally feeling unwell.
- Fresh bleeding from the wound that does not stop with gentle pressure.

## Pain Management
- Take prescribed pain medication on schedule; do not skip doses and do not
  exceed the prescribed dose.
- Report pain that is uncontrolled, worsening, or out of proportion to the
  surgery. Sudden severe pain can indicate bleeding, infection, or a clot.
- Elevating the operated area and using cold packs (as approved) can reduce
  swelling and discomfort.

## Medication Adherence
- Take all prescribed medications (antibiotics, analgesics, anticoagulants) at
  the correct time and dose. Antibiotics must be finished even if the patient
  feels better.
- Blood-thinning medication increases bleeding risk — report unusual bruising,
  bleeding gums, or dark stools to the doctor.
- Maintain an up-to-date medication list and share it with every care provider.

## Activity and Movement
- Follow the surgeon's activity restrictions, including limits on lifting,
  driving, and bending. Typically avoid heavy lifting (more than 4-5 kg) for the
  first few weeks.
- Mobilise early as advised (short, frequent walks) to reduce the risk of blood
  clots, unless bed rest was ordered.
- Stop any activity that causes sharp pain and rest.

## Nutrition and Hydration
- Eat a balanced diet rich in protein (to support healing) and stay well
  hydrated. Drink water rather than sugary drinks.
- Avoid smoking and alcohol while recovering; both delay healing and increase
  infection and bleeding risk.

## When to Seek Emergency Help
Seek emergency care immediately for:
- Chest pain, shortness of breath, or sudden difficulty breathing (possible
  blood clot in the lungs).
- Sudden leg swelling, pain, or tenderness (possible deep vein thrombosis).
- Heavy, uncontrolled bleeding from the wound.
- Confusion, high fever, or inability to keep fluids down.

## Recovery Expectations
- Healing progress is individual; small variations are normal. Recovery is
  considered on track when pain, swelling, and wound drainage gradually improve
  day over day.
- A monitoring agent should compare each check-in against previous ones and
  escalate if the reported symptoms are worsening, if medication is being
  missed, or if any warning sign above appears.
